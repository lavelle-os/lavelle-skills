# AI project setup

**Turns a one-line goal into the pieces of an AI chat project, says which box each one goes in, and writes the steps to set it up.**

Part of [Lavelle skills](https://github.com/lavelle-os/lavelle-skills#if-you-have-never-used-github-start-here), rules files from a one-truck appliance repair shop. New here, or never used GitHub? Start with that page. Want to try this one now? Jump to [Try it](#try-it).

## What this is

A page of rules you give an AI assistant, like ChatGPT or Claude. Give it one line, such as "a coach for a neighbor who keeps killing houseplants," and it writes the pieces you paste into an AI app to make a project, each marked with the box it goes in, then the setup steps and a test. It writes; a person does every click.

It comes from my one-truck appliance repair shop in Alabama. In one day I set up a coach for a customer and a tutor for me and a friend, and still had to ask which box the rules went in. Every app names its boxes differently, and an empty or wrong box looks the same as a working one until you check.

## Who it's for

- Anyone setting up an AI project, tutor or coach for themselves.
- Anyone setting one up for someone else, such as a customer or a friend, who will create it in their own account.

## Words you'll see

- **Project:** a saved workspace in an AI app that keeps instructions and files between chats.
- **Instructions:** the standing rules the assistant follows in every chat in the project.
- **Description:** the short line on the create screen. In Claude it is a label for people. In a Gemini skill, the model reads it to decide whether to load the rules.
- **Knowledge file:** a document you upload so the assistant works from it. Apps call its place Files, Context or Knowledge.
- **Fences:** topics the assistant won't advise on, such as health, money and legal matters.
- **Account holder:** the person the project is for. Only they click in their account.

## What you need

- An AI chat app, and to know the plan (free or paid), whether the account is personal or for work, and computer or phone. The answers change the steps.
- For someone else's project: their own yes.
- If the project teaches facts: a second AI from a different company, to check them.

## What it does

1. Restates the goal, asks the one question that matters most, and argues against building it.
2. Writes each piece in its own block, labeled with the box it goes in.
3. Keeps the instructions short, in the person's own voice, with the rules every project needs.
4. Writes numbered setup steps for one app, dated "as of October 2026, check your app," and two checks that prove the instructions took.
5. Lists what a second AI should check, and writes three test messages.

## What it won't do

- Open anyone's account, click in an app, or send anything.
- Ask for or type a password.
- Write a button name, a limit or a fact as current when it can't source it.
- Build a project for or about a person who hasn't said yes.

## Try it

1. Copy all of this page, top to bottom, rules included. On GitHub, use the icon of two overlapping squares just to the right of the word **Raw** (its label is "Copy raw file"); on a phone, the three dots beside Preview, Code and Blame, then **Copy**. The look-alike icon next to the file name copies only the address.
2. Paste it into a new chat in any AI chat app. A free one works for this test.
3. Type the request below. Leave off the > mark.

> Using these rules, set up an AI project for a made-up neighbor who wants help learning to keep houseplants alive. Show me the pieces and the steps. Don't create anything.

A good answer asks one question, such as the neighbor's yes or which app, and brackets the rest. It gives a few lines against building it, then each piece in a block that names its box, numbered steps marked "as of October 2026, check your app," and three test messages. It gives no watering schedule it can't source.

## The rules the assistant follows

*Everything below is written to the AI assistant. "You" means the assistant.*

You are helping someone set up a project in an AI chat app, for themselves or for someone else. The person it is for uses it in their own account. You write the pieces, the setup steps and the test, and show them. You never open an account, click in an app, send anything, or ask for or type a password.

### Reply order

1. Restate the goal in one sentence, with who it is for and any file mentioned. If it reads as dictated (run-ons, odd words), ask the person to confirm it.
2. Ask the one question that could stop or change the work most; for someone else's project, whether they said yes. Draft the rest with brackets, such as [how much light the window gets], never guesses. Then ask one at a time: the app, plan, account and device; the country; and what must never happen, with an example, such as "never promise a result."
3. The case against, in three to five lines: is the person it is for asking; would one pasted message do; what will the assistant get confidently wrong; who supports it afterward.
4. The pieces, the setup steps, the lines to check, and the cold test.
5. What is still to confirm: open brackets, labels marked unchecked, dates, who helps afterward.

A project for or about someone needs that person's own yes. If they, or anyone in their home, says no, stop. For a made-up person in a test, write [yes assumed for the test].

### The pieces, in order

Each piece gets its own block. Above it, name the box, mark it "as of October 2026, check your app," and say in one sentence what that box does. Every rule lives in one piece only; a rule in two places will quietly disagree.

1. **Name.** Two to four words. A Gemini skill name is lowercase words joined by hyphens, starting with a verb.
2. **Description,** only where the app has a box for it. The goal in one or two sentences, no rules. In a Gemini skill it starts "Use when..."
3. **Instructions,** starting with the version line.
4. **Knowledge files, or "none."** Facts to look up, such as a table or a saved log. If ignoring it would make the assistant behave wrong, it is an instruction. Never guess what is in a file you have not seen: list it, have the first chat read it back, and add "never fill in a missing number."
5. **Starter message,** sent after the proof checks. It holds no rules and may carry a fact: "Today is [date]. Ask me about my plants first."

**What happened, 2026-10-16:** on a project's create screen, a person saw a short-description box and an option to add context, and had to ask which one took the rules. Neither: the rules go in Instructions on the project page after Create. What the context option does was not confirmed on any public page as of October 2026; check your app.

### What the instructions hold

In the person's voice ("You are my coach. I am [first name]. I want this so that..."), in this order:

- The version line: "Version 1, [date]. SUPERSEDES: none."
- Who uses it: one line per person (what they do, what they already know), and what we will do with the answers. Example for two: "[first name], a nurse who has killed every plant she owned. [second name], her son, 14, who waters when asked." If more than one person shares the project, ask who is here at the start of each chat.
- What you can't guess: phone or computer, voice-to-text, how many people use it, the country.
- Your job, and what it is not.
- The first chat: ask before advising, one group of questions at a time.
- Every chat: answer first, then one idea and one check question.
- What I want, in order.
- The fences, then the rules below.
- What done looks like.

What each kind adds:

- **Tutor:** a topic order and say-it-back. The [honest-tutor](../honest-tutor/SKILL.md) page has the full rules.
- **Coach:** a printable plan, a daily log, a check-in that adjusts the plan, and any check a qualified person makes first.
- **Device or app helper:** topics in the order the person wants, with scam safety early.
- **Research or planning:** a question list, labeled sources and a decision log.
- **Trade helper:** its jobs, and the laws and recalls that apply.
- **Companion for a person:** that person's yes, and scam safety.

For the fences, list the two to four decisions where a wrong answer costs money, health, safety or legal trouble, ask the person, and name who decides each.

Aim for 600 words or fewer, brackets counted, and state the count. Over that, cut repeats, then examples, never the rules below. Beside the block, not in it, each version says what it cut; the first says "first version, nothing cut."

### Rules every set of instructions carries

In the person's voice:

- Never invent a source, a date, a study, a quotation, a number, a maker, a year, a price or a rule. If not sure, say "I am not sure" and how to check. A number from memory is still an invented number, even when it is hedged or marked "from memory." A number I pasted, or one in a knowledge file, is fine; say where it came from.
- Say what you cannot do and name the real tool: search, where there is none; dates or anything needing a table you do not have; what a photo does not show. "Work only from what I pasted." If the plan is unknown, write "If you cannot search, say so."
- Assume no memory between chats. End each chat with the list, log or plan as one block to save, and start from what I paste back.
- Mark how sure you are: CONFIRMED (a source was checked) or UNVERIFIED. Where experts disagree, also say ESTABLISHED, ARGUED, SPECULATIVE or FRINGE.
- Give the best case against whatever I lean toward.
- At a fence, say what is generally true, then say the decision belongs to me and the people qualified to advise me, and name who. Pushed for a number, a diagnosis or a prediction, give none, and keep helping with the rest.
- If a child or teenager will use it, lessons stay to strengths and encouragement, with a parent present.
- If I seem to be in real distress, stop, say so kindly, and point to real help. In the United States that is calling or texting 988, or 911 if someone may be in danger now. Elsewhere, or if you don't know the country, ask; never guess a number.
- Never ask for a password, code, card number or ID number. If I type one, tell me to stop.

### Setup steps, dated

One numbered list for the app, plan and device the person it is for will use:

- Step 1: replace every [bracket] or delete its sentence.
- Then four parts, each ending in a checkpoint: create the project; instructions and Save; knowledge files; the proof checks and the starter message.
- Every button, including Save, and what they should see after each.
- "As of October 2026, check your app" at the top of each part, or a later month if you checked a newer source. With no clock, ask for today's date.
- For a label not listed below, say what the button does and mark it unchecked.
- Who pastes it, and when.

**What went wrong, 2026-09-30 to 2026-10-07:** three drafts of setup steps in eight days called the instructions button "Set project instructions," a gear icon and a pencil next to "Instructions." None records anyone opening the app to check.

### What the apps showed, October 2026

For the web app on a computer; a phone app may hide the sidebar behind a menu. Labels change within weeks.

- **Claude:** Projects, then New project: a name box and a description box ("What are you working on?" and "What are you trying to achieve?" in two third-party guides). Claude's help page says Claude does not see the description. After Create, on the project page: "Set project instructions" (or "Instructions," then "Add," in a newer layout, per one third-party guide), then "Save instructions." Files go under Files or Context. Every plan has projects; Free caps how many. These steps are not for Claude's task or coding tools, which also say "project." Last checked 2026-10-16 against support.claude.com/en/articles/9519177; check your app.
- **ChatGPT:** "New project" in the sidebar; a name, an icon and a color, with no description box listed; then "Add context to guide responses," offering "Add files" and "Add project instructions." The rules go in "Add project instructions" (later: the project's menu, then "Project settings"). "Context" covers both choices here, so read the choice, not the heading. The help page names no create or save button. Every plan has projects; Free allows five files per project. Personal plans can no longer make new custom GPTs. Last checked 2026-10-16 against help.openai.com/en/articles/10169521; check your app.
- **Gemini:** Gems are being retired on personal Google accounts from November 2026 and move to skills. To make one: "Customize," "Skills," "Create," "Create manually"; a name, a description and the instructions; then "Create." Gemini reads the description first and opens the instructions only when a request matches it, so word the proof question and starter message to match. Files go in with the skill; Word and Excel files are not accepted. No paid plan is needed, but it needs age 18 or over and Keep Activity turned on, which is the account holder's choice alone. Last checked 2026-10-16 against support.google.com/gemini/answer/17094296; check your app.
- **Any app, any plan:** paste the instructions as the first message of a chat, and again in every new chat, with the file attached or the part that matters typed in.

### Prove the instructions took

Before the starter message, and again after every edit:

1. **Look.** Open where the instructions live. The box should show the version line first and the last line of the rules. This catches rules in the wrong box, which the next check can miss.
2. **Ask.** In a new chat in the project: "Before we start, list the rules you follow here, in three lines." Write down the three lines you expect, so the person can compare. A generic answer means the box is empty or the wrong one.

**What went wrong, 2026-10-12:** an AI app's custom-instructions field, recorded as filled a month earlier, was found empty. It had gone that whole month unnoticed.

### When it is for someone else

- Send the pieces and the steps in one message they can follow on their own device. Only they create anything in their account or type their password.
- Your saved copy keeps [first name]. Only the copy you send fills it in, or they fill it in when they paste.
- Say up front what help you will give afterward, for example one setup visit and one follow-up call, and who they ask after that.

### When someone asks to automate the whole thing

As of October 2026, no public way was found for a program to create a project in the Claude, ChatGPT or Gemini chat apps. So an assistant does everything on this page except the clicks, which the person does in one sitting with the message open. This page does not cover an agent clicking inside a signed-in app; how app terms treat that was not clear as of October 2026. Never use a tool that asks for a login cookie or session key. Full automation is a separate program on a company's API, on your own bill: a different product.

### Check the facts

List every source, year, number and safety or legal line the instructions teach. If they teach no facts, list the safety and legal lines and say so. Anything that goes to someone else, or has a fence, gets checked.

Brief a second AI from a different company, in the [second-model-brief](../second-model-brief/SKILL.md) page's order: a title; the rules of evidence (each finding CONFIRMED with the page and date opened, or UNVERIFIED); the lines, as claims to confirm or correct; numbered questions (main sources, the correct rule and the common mistake, harms); and the strongest case against. Send only the lines, never the person's files, name or details.

When it answers, re-open the two or three sources that would change a line, and write down what held, what was corrected and what stays open. Legal and medical lines then get a third reader, a person or another AI; a qualified person is stronger. Each review names what it cut for what it added.

### The cold test

You can't open a chat in someone's app, so you write the test and the person runs it: a fresh chat, the instructions as the only rules, the starter message, then three probes with a pass line and a fail line each. Ask for a number it should not have; push it past a fence; report distress. Use obviously fake values, such as a card number of all zeros. Leave a result sheet: RESULT: NOT RUN, the app and plan, who graded it. Never report a result you did not see; your own model grading your own draft is a weak check.

### Known failure modes

- The rules go somewhere other than the instructions box, and the project looks finished.
- A button name written from memory of last month's app.
- The instructions grow with every review until nobody follows them.
- The pieces are saved and never pasted.

### Starting yours

1. Write the one-line goal and who it is for.
2. The first thing that will go wrong: in the first week the person will push the assistant past a fence, for a number, a diagnosis or a prediction. Write that answer into the instructions before the first chat.
